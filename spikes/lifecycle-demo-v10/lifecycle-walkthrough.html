One application · six stages · nothing hidden

Rent assistance application

Watch one small government application go from a sentence somebody said, to a signed release, to a change request that rebuilds it — and see exactly what the machinery allowed, refused, and lost along the way.

The brief it started from

  1. B1A tenant behind on rent tells us about their household and what went wrong.
  2. B2Before it counts, they sign a declaration that what they said is true.
  3. B3They get a receipt they can show a landlord or a court.
  4. B4A caseworker works through the queue and decides.

What we set out to prove, and what happened

5 of 6 met
BAR 1 Met

Everything traces back to the brief

Met, and narrower than it sounds: only 1 of the 4 journey entries is mounted on a page at all, so the unit-to-page hop holds over 1 of 4. The other three are unmountable on a Definition-bearing bundle — `APP-GRAPH-SURFACE-EXPERIENCE-UNIT-DEFINITION` requires any route mounting a unit to also carry a `definition-form` slot for the Experience's `targetDefinition`. The chain is connected; it is not four parallel chains.

Each hop from a brief sentence to a page, a question and a rendered field is either an edge the substrate itself recorded, or a reference physically present in the exported files.

BAR 5 Not met

The designer's edits survive a rebuild

After the change request rebuilds the app, both hand-made edits are still there, put there by the substrate's own three-way merge.

No regeneration merge exists to run. No merge entry point exists. 332 runtime exports across 5 substrate packages were enumerated and scanned twice. Zero carry a regeneration-merge entry-point name (/regenerat|three.?way|mergereport|mergedraft|sourceanchor/i). 7 carry the merge spec's own §3/§9 identity vocabulary (/anchor|x-?generation|generationmarker|designeredit|preservation/i) — emptyAnchorMappingsDocument, validateAnchorMappingsDocument, appendAnchorMapping, anchorString, anchorsOf, computeAnchorGroups, reanchorEntries, all in @formspec-org/studio-core. So the merge's identity and rename primitives ship; the merge that would compose them does not. The spec is Draft 1.0.0-draft.1, the report schema ships, 17 three-way fixture scenarios ship carrying expected merge OUTPUTS, 10 of which assert that a designer-only value survives — and NO test in the repo reads any of those expected outputs. The conformance suite collects 17 tests under `-k regeneration`, all of them in tests/conformance/spec/test_regeneration_merge_report_schema.py — which validates the report SCHEMA and never runs a merge. 9 further files name the corpus without executing it. Both designer edits are therefore lost on rebuild: DESIGNER-EDIT-1 (designer-inserted), DESIGNER-EDIT-2 (designer-modified). This was the pre-registered prediction, and it is ADR 0159's own falsifier firing — the moat is specified, fixtured, and unbuilt.
